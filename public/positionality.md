# Positionality Statement

I am Liz McKenna, the instructor who wrote this focus-group protocol, taught
the class whose students produced the data, and now analyzes and publishes the
results. Every one of those roles shapes what gets seen and what doesn't.

The most obvious asymmetry: the students in the room know I will read the
transcript and I grade them. That almost certainly muted certain kinds of
candor — how heavily they lean on AI, which course policies they quietly
ignore, how they really feel about assignments I designed. No cleanup pass on
the transcript can recover what was never said out loud.

Less obvious but just as real: I wrote the protocol, which means I chose which
questions to ask. The deductive codebook the analysis leans on is keyed to
those questions. The analysis can only find what the protocol made sayable.
An inductive pass done cold might surface categories this instrument cannot.
I did a small inductive demo on one section — that is a demo, not a method.

**What I did not do and should have, if this were a paper rather than a
teaching artifact:**

- No second human coder on any pass. Every claim resting on "I coded this
  way and Claude coded that way" has one human in the loop: me.
- No inter-rater reliability computed. No κ, no percent agreement. The
  disagreements between me and Claude that the site shows are not a
  validity check — they are a provocation for students to look at.
- No member checking. I have not gone back to the participants to ask
  whether the themes feel true to their experience.
- The inductive pass is scoped to roughly an hour of my time. A real
  inductive analysis would be weeks.

**What the site does to compensate — partially:**

- Publishes the raw disagreements rather than a reconciled "final" read.
- Labels the inductive section as a demo and names what rigor would require.
- Surfaces this statement on the first scroll, not buried in a footer.
- Invites readers — other students, colleagues, faculty — to react to every
  section, and takes their reactions seriously as a form of distributed
  member checking.

None of that is the same as a second coder. It is honest about what it is.

**On why I'm publishing at all:**

The pedagogical goal is to show students what qualitative coding looks like
— its affordances, its failure modes, what LLMs change about the picture, and
where you can still get away with being fast. A site that silently performs
rigor it doesn't have would teach the opposite of what I want students to
learn. A site that names its gaps aloud might teach something useful about
method.

If any participant reads this and wants a quote removed or a theme
re-examined, please email me. That remains true after publication.

— Liz
